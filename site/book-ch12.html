Leading the Intelligent Factory:  Chapter 12

Score your plant

Where you stand on reliability, quality and cost, and your first step.

Draft for review

Spread 1

Ten minutes, six rows, one honest look

You have seen the six decisions at work in every chapter. Now score your own plant on each one.

For each row of the scorecard, pick the statement that sounds most like your plant today, not where you want it to be. Then ask a superintendent and an operator to do the same. Where the answers differ is worth a conversation.

The result is not a grade. It is a sentence that says where to start, and a first step.

Then read the result by pressure. Each pressure is only as strong as the weakest decision behind it. Start with the weakest. If two are tied, take reliability before quality, and quality before cost.

Figure 12.1: the scorecard

Six decisions, five levels

What should we run, and when?The schedule follows the order book and habit.We know which transitions hurt and avoid the worst.Sequence and campaign rules are written down and followed.Sequence and stock levels are set from measured transition cost.Each campaign’s losses update the sequence rules.
How fast should we run?Rate is set by whoever is on shift.We know our constraints and respect them.Rate targets by product, followed by every crew.Rate is set by margin and cost, not capability alone.Rate rules update as markets and equipment change.
Where should everything else be set?Every crew runs its own recipe.We know which settings cause trouble.Centerlines by product and rate that crews run to.Targets tested against cost, not just consistency.Targets refined after every review and upset.
When should we intervene?We find out when it breaks.Someone reviews the data daily and owns the findings.Clear limits and responses, the same on every shift.Alarms and limits tuned so only what matters gets through.Each intervention tunes the limits that follow.
What should we fix first?Whatever is loudest gets fixed.A ranked list exists, but it is long.Priorities follow agreed rules by risk and value.Hours and capital go where constraint and value say.Results feed back into how we rank next time.

Mark one box in each row. Pick the statement that sounds most like your plant today, not where you want it to be.

Spread 2

Your first step

Find your weakest decision and its level. The step beside that level is the place to start. Each step points to the chapter that shows it at work.

Do not skip a level. A cost study on a process that trips every week will not hold, and a target that no crew runs to will not pay.
