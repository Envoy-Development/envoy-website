Expanding Into Food

Start with the question your team needs to answer.

Why does one operating period perform differently from another? What changed before an interruption? Which conditions accompany higher losses or greater energy use? These are useful starting points for a conversation about your production process.

Envoy’s foundation is engineering-led monitoring and analysis in pulp and paper. As we expand into food, we are looking for focused applications where that approach can be adapted to a plant’s equipment, operating context, and production priorities.

The first step is to understand the process with your team. We then assess the available data, define a manageable scope, and agree on the result that would make the work valuable.

A useful first conversation

  • Which process or line needs more consistent performance?
  • Where do losses or interruptions create the most work?
  • What production, quality, and utility data is available?
  • Which differences between products or operating periods matter?

Applications to Explore

Connect operating conditions with production outcomes.

These are potential starting points for scoping together, based on the process and data at your facility.

Process consistency

Explore how temperatures, flows, pressures, and other measured conditions differ between stable periods and periods that require more operator attention.

Material losses and yield

Compare production and material-use records with process history to identify patterns worth investigating around waste, rework, or changing yield.

Production interruptions

Review the sequence of operating changes around a recurring stop or slowdown and help the engineering team develop a focused investigation.

Energy and utilities

Examine energy or utility use alongside production conditions, with attention to operating rate, product differences, and the quality of available measurements.

The scope of any initial engagement would be agreed with your team after reviewing process fit, available data, and the engineering support required.

A Focused Starting Point

One process. One useful objective.

A narrow initial application makes it easier to establish context, assess the analysis, and decide where further work would help.

01 / UNDERSTAND

Define the operating question.

Choose a recurring issue or improvement objective. Identify the process boundaries, the people who will use the findings, and the decisions they need to make.

02 / ASSESS

Review the available evidence.

Look at signal coverage, measurement quality, production records, and relevant operating differences. Agree on what can reasonably be learned from that information.

03 / EVALUATE

Agree on what useful means.

Set a practical objective for the initial work and a review point with your team. Use what you learn to decide on the next application.
