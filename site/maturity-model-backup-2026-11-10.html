The Intelligent Factory:   The Maturity Model

Where is your operation on the curve?

Unstable, Stable, Repeatable, Optimized, Adaptive. Every operation sits somewhere on this five-level curve. Knowing where you are matters more than knowing where you're headed, because it tells you what will work next and what will fail.

Take the Assessment Intelligent Factory: Start Here

The Model

Five levels of operational maturity.

Maturity is not how much technology an operation owns. It is how decisions get made. Each level is a real shift in how the operation detects, decides and learns, and each has a trap that keeps teams stuck there.

LEVEL 1

Unstable

Firefighting. Problems find you.

The trap: Heroic firefighting becomes normal.

LEVEL 2

Stable

You know what is wrong and you are fixing it.

The trap: Finding more problems is not improving.

LEVEL 3

Repeatable

Everyone runs it the same way.

The trap: A standard on paper that nobody runs to.

LEVEL 4

Optimized

You know the best way, not just a consistent way.

The trap: Optimizing one area at the expense of the whole.

LEVEL 5

Adaptive

What you learn gets baked back in.

The trap: Confusing adaptation with automation.

Why This Matters

Most industrial AI fails because it is sold at the wrong stage.

You cannot centerline a process that is broken. You cannot optimize a process that is not repeatable. Vendors sell optimization into unstable plants, it fails, and the organization concludes that AI does not work in manufacturing. The conclusion is wrong. The sequencing was.

Stable before repeatableA process that breaks unpredictably cannot be standardized. Fix first.
Repeatable before optimizedThree crews, three ways to run: there is no single point to optimize.
Optimized before adaptiveLearning compounds only when there is a standard for it to update.
Adaptive keeps it allStop learning and the plant slides back down the curve.

Moving Up

What actually has to change.

Each step up the curve is a change in how the plant works, not a change in what it owns.

  1. 12
    Unstable to Stable

    Someone reviews the operation every day, and every finding has a name on it. Not a dashboard somebody could look at.

  2. 23
    Stable to Repeatable

    The best-known way to run is written down by product and rate, and crews run to it. Most plants skip this step.

  3. 34
    Repeatable to Optimized

    The standard gets tested against cost, not just against consistency. Consistent and expensive is still expensive.

  4. 45
    Optimized to Adaptive

    What each event teaches is written back into the standard. When a problem returns, the second investigation costs less.

Where Are You?

A plant is rarely at one level.

Most operations are further along on some decisions than others. That is why the assessment scores each of the six decisions against these five levels, instead of giving the plant one grade.

“Repeatable on how fast we run, unstable on when we intervene. Start there.”
