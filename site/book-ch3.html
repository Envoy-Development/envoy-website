Leading the Intelligent Factory:  Chapter 3

What AI can and cannot do

AI improves the decisions behind your results. It does not own them.

Draft for review

Spread 1

Better decisions, not a new boss

You are measured on three things: reliability, quality and cost. AI cannot move any of them on its own. Every result on your scorecard comes from a decision someone made on a shift. AI earns its place by making those decisions better.

What AI does well. It reads every tag, every night, against its own history. It finds patterns across thousands of tags that no person has time to check. And it remembers every past case, so the same symptom does not start from zero.

What it cannot do. It cannot own a decision. It cannot fix a valve, make a bad measurement good, or stabilize a process that breaks every shift. It does not know that a crew runs light on Sundays, or why a grade is always hard. Your people know that.

What this means for you. Give AI the seeing, the sorting and the remembering. Keep the deciding with the people who run the plant, and give them a short list instead of a long screen.

The next six chapters take the three pressures in order: reliability first, then quality, then cost. That is the order that works, for the reason Chapter 2 gave.

In practice. Every Envoy finding goes to a named engineer at your plant, with what we saw and what we would check first. The call stays with your team.
