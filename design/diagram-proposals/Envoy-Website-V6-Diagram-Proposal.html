Give each page one visual job.

The homepage shows the customer benefit. The platform page explains the shared foundation. Module pages show how a question becomes a useful decision. Industry pages make that reasoning concrete through recognizable equipment and process relationships.

Replace repeated sections.

Each illustration is paired with the cards or text it should replace. Keep the photography, real report examples, a short explanation, and a clear next step. The goal is a more understandable page with less reading.

A consistent visual language, with room for different stories.

Use Envoy’s green, mint, and teal palette, open white space, restrained linework, and one amber highlight to direct attention. Equipment and relationships do the explaining; labels stay short. These are editable SVG illustrations and remain sharp when enlarged.

The approved hero photographs provide the human presence. These diagrams belong in the body of each page, where visitors are trying to understand how Envoy helps.

  • One main diagram per page. Add a secondary visual only when it answers a distinct question.
  • Make the mobile composition deliberate. Reflow the story into readable stages instead of shrinking a wide diagram.
  • Keep explanations accessible. Use an accessible title, description, nearby text, and meaning beyond color alone.
  • Use motion sparingly. A selectable highlight may help, but each diagram should explain itself as a still image.

Recommended first implementation pass

Begin with the homepage, Reliability Reporting, and pulp and paper pages. Together they establish three useful patterns: customer benefit, evidence-to-action, and a process relationship. Improving and moving the existing Smart Centerlines diagram is a straightforward companion change. Apply the same visual language to the remaining pages once these examples feel right.

This file contains the complete proposal and nine vector studies. It does not change the Version 5 site, publish a new site, or package the website for release.

Process references and illustration scope

The layouts are original design proposals. Process equipment is simplified, and all plotted traces are illustrative. Food and specialty-chemicals concepts describe potential applications. The references below ground the process examples; they do not substantiate Envoy customer results or product capabilities.

  • TAPPI — Paper Manufacturing Courses: stock preparation, forming, wet pressing, and drying.
  • Valmet — Press sections: press dewatering and its relationship to sheet dryness and drying capacity.
  • Tetra Pak Orange Book — Processing at the juice packer: blending, heat treatment, filling, and a floating buffer connected off the main line in the example described.
  • De Dietrich — Studies & Simulations: reactor mixing, heat transfer, and heating/cooling context. The four-phase sequence shown here is an illustrative design choice.
